How it works

Geometry, not guesswork.

The Windows face detector reports six facial keypoints — never head angles. Those keypoints are enough to recover the angles analytically.

01 / YAW

Where the head points

Model the head as a cylinder: nose at the front, ear points at ±90°. The nose's position t between the two ears collapses to a tangent, so the angle falls straight out.

yaw = atan((t − 0.5) × 2)

02 / PITCH

Calibrated to your face

How far your nose sits below your eye line is a fact about you, not about your attention — a fixed threshold misfires on real people. SafeScreen learns your neutral value and measures drift from it.

deviation = ratio − learned_neutral

03 / DECISION

Weighted toward hiding

Hiding takes one bad frame. Revealing takes several consecutive good ones. Flickering to "protected" is an annoyance; flickering to "visible" is a privacy failure. They are not treated as equal.

framesToProtect ≤ framesToReveal

The part most tools would hide

Where your camera frames actually go.

They touch the disk. Briefly. Here is exactly how.

The Windows camera plugin has no streaming API — the only way to get a frame is to take a photo, and it writes that photo to disk before any of SafeScreen's code can see it. By default that destination is your Pictures folder, which on most machines syncs to OneDrive. A gaze detector sampling several times a second would therefore upload thousands of photographs of your face.

So SafeScreen ships a forked plugin whose one change redirects captures to %TEMP%\SafeScreenFrames — not a sync target — and then overwrites each file with zeros and deletes it the instant it has been read, on every code path, sweeping any survivors at shutdown.

Frames stay on your machine and do not outlive their read. But they are written, and zeroing is best-effort on copy-on-write filesystems and wear-levelled SSDs. Removing disk writes entirely needs a native capture path, which is the top item on the roadmap. Until then, this is written down rather than glossed over. Full detail in SECURITY.md →

Scope

What it is not.

A privacy tool that overstates itself is worse than none at all.

  • ✗Not a lock screen. It authenticates nobody and will not stop someone using your keyboard. That is what Win+L is for.
  • ✗Not multi-monitor. Yet. The blackout covers the primary display only; other screens stay visible. Roadmap item two.
  • ✗Not face recognition. It knows a face is present and roughly where it points — not whose it is. Shoulder-surfer detection triggers on any second face, including one on a poster.
  • ✗Not signed. Certificates cost more than this project has, so SmartScreen will warn you. Verify the checksum instead.
