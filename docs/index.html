An open-source privacy screen protector for Windows.

SafeScreen watches through your webcam to work out whether you are actually looking at your display — and blacks it out the moment you are not. Or the moment someone else appears over your shoulder.

How camera frames are handled

The Windows camera plugin has no streaming API, so each frame is written to disk before SafeScreen can see it — and by default that means your Pictures folder, which usually syncs to OneDrive. SafeScreen ships a forked plugin that redirects those writes into private temp storage, then overwrites and deletes each file the instant it is read.

Frames stay on your machine and do not outlive their read — but they are written briefly, and that is worth knowing before you install a tool that watches you. The complete account is in SECURITY.md →

What it does

Look at your screen and nothing happens. Stop looking, and it goes black.

👤 Real head-pose detection

Not "is there a face somewhere". Yaw and roll are recovered from facial geometry, and your neutral head position is learned over time.

👥 Shoulder-surfer detection

A second face in view protects the screen — even while you are still looking at it yourself.

🔒 Fails closed

Camera unplugged, detector wedged, frames stopped arriving? It protects. It never stays exposed because something broke.

📴 No network at all

No telemetry, no analytics, no update check, no account. There isn't even an HTTP dependency in the project.

How it works

Every frame is destroyed the instant it has been read.

  • Head pose from keypoints. The detector reports six facial points, not angles. Modelling the head as a cylinder — nose at the front, ear points at ±90° — the nose's position between them gives t = 0.5 + 0.5·tan θ, so yaw falls out as atan((t − 0.5)·2).
  • Pitch is calibrated to your face. How far your nose sits below your eye line is a fact about you, not about your attention. A fixed threshold misfires on real people, so SafeScreen learns your neutral value.
  • Hiding and revealing are weighted differently. Hiding takes one bad frame; revealing takes several consecutive good ones. Flickering to "protected" is an annoyance. Flickering to "visible" is a privacy failure.

Verify what you download

Binaries are unsigned, so SmartScreen will warn you. Rather than clicking through blindly, check the hash. 

Get-FileHash .\SafeScreen-windows-x64.zip -Algorithm SHA256

Compare against SHA256SUMS.txt in the release. Every release is built by GitHub Actions from a tagged commit — never uploaded from a developer's machine — and carries a provenance attestation: 

gh attestation verify SafeScreen-windows-x64.zip --repo kv4u/safescreen

What it is not

A privacy tool that overstates itself is worse than none.

  • Not a lock screen. It does not authenticate anyone and will not stop someone using your keyboard. Use Win+L for that.
  • Only the primary monitor is covered. Other displays stay visible when you look away. It's the number two item on the roadmap.
  • It does not know who you are. It knows a face is present and roughly where it's pointed. Shoulder-surfer detection triggers on any second face — including one on a poster.
  • Frames touch disk briefly. In private temp storage, erased immediately — but written. See the notice at the top of this page.
